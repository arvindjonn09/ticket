# Business calendar automation

Plan sections 22 (items 3 and 5) and 24: administrator settings at `GET/POST /api/admin/automation` configure working weekdays (Sunday 0–Saturday 6), up to 366 explicit holiday dates, and reminder/escalation/closure thresholds from 1–365 business days. Escalation cannot precede reminders. Settings require a current administrator MFA session, optimistic revision and CSRF on writes. No row means disabled; proposed defaults are Monday–Friday, no holidays, 2/5/5 days. Enabling explicitly authorizes applying the chosen policy to existing tickets.

The calendar uses the tenant's current IANA timezone and original local time, including daylight-saving changes. Business days exclude the starting date, nonworking weekdays and holidays. These are date-based timers, not support-hour SLAs. Calendar edits intentionally affect outstanding timers.

The existing durable outbox worker polls active enabled tenant IDs through a metadata-only function, then rechecks each ticket inside a tenant transaction and the existing configuration lock. Approval stage timing starts at its version's evaluation audit, or the manager's decision for a subsequent VP stage. Live policy, state, stage, approver eligibility and version are rechecked. Reminder and escalation each emit one audited outbox event per ticket/version/stage. Email delivery rechecks recipients and never grants approval authority. Escalation alerts give administrators a generic configuration link; queue owners require active same-group membership. Held and terminal tickets produce no timer actions.

Resolved incidents complete from their latest recorded resolution transition, never from a message or unrelated update timestamp. A reopen and later resolve starts a new period. No resolution audit means no automatic closure. Closure, revision increment, audit, unique action record and outbox event commit atomically. Repeated and concurrent ticks cannot repeat actions. SMTP retries do not execute business actions.

Saving names the saving administrator as the accountable actor. A disabled or demoted responsible administrator pauses all automation until another administrator saves settings. Audit detail identifies the `business_calendar` trigger, responsible administrator and calendar revision; no fictional system user is created.

Tenant deletion removes actions before tickets and settings before users. The simple poll scans active pending/resolved tickets per enabled tenant; use indexed due timestamps if measured volumes outgrow this initial operating model.
